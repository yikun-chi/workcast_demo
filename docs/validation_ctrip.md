# Validation: work from home in a call centre (Ctrip, QJE 2015)

Numeric claims are generated by `scripts/build_ctrip_validation.py` from `runs/ctrip/evaluation.json` and `instances/ctrip/calibration/`.

## Design

Ctrip randomly assigned 131 of 249 volunteer call-centre employees to work from home four days a week from 6 December 2010; the other 118 stayed in the office. Each employee is a digital twin built from their pre-experiment characteristics and their own 2010 weekly record. Twins' weekly calls and performance scores move with home working by a published meta-analytic effect; their weekly quit risk combines the 2010 exit rate of call-centre staff with published tenure, age and turnover-intent effects. Every prior cites work published before the experiment's results appeared (February 2013). The simulation runs 50 times; point predictions are the mean and ranges cover 90% of runs. Scored weeks are 2010 week 50 to 2011 week 33; week 49, when the experiment began, is a transition week and is not scored.

## Development on 2010 data

The twin calibration was chosen before the experiment period was read, on two pseudo-changes inside 2010 (weeks 201027 and 201037). Rule: level estimator: lowest sum over pseudo-changes of person-week MAE (performance and log calls) relative to the plain 2010 mean; exit hazard: lowest mean absolute error in the share leaving among staff with at least 20 (26) observed weeks.

## Where the simulation wins and loses

Closer than every other prediction: Effect of working at home on the overall performance score; Quit by the end of the experiment, home group; Quit by the end of the experiment, office group.

Further than the best other prediction: Effect of working at home on weekly calls answered (best: Earlier research); Effect of working at home on quits (best: Earlier research); Weekly calls per order taker, home group (best: Same weeks last year); Weekly calls per order taker, office group (best: Recent trend); Overall performance score, home group (best: No change); Overall performance score, office group (best: Recent trend).

- Effect of working at home on weekly calls answered: the simulation's +7.6% is inside the experiment's 95% interval (+4.8% to +22.0%).
- Effect of working at home on quits: the simulation's -1.3 pp is outside the experiment's 95% interval (-29.4 pp to -8.1 pp).
- Quits in the office group were 34.7% against 17.5% predicted from the 2010 exit rate; in the home group 16.0% against 16.2%. The closest other prediction for the office group was No change at 17.3%.

## Alternative: two home days a week

With the same 20 seeds, the simulated effect on calls falls from +7.9% to +5.2% and the effect on quits from -1.0 pp to -0.4 pp. This is a simulated alternative, not an observed estimate; its size rests on the sourced two-day share of the effect (`intensity_share_2d`).

## Limitations

This is a retrospective evaluation. The design was chosen by people who knew the published result in outline, although every prior takes the published meta-analytic value as printed and the specification was chosen on 2010 data only. The 2010 exit hazard comes from staff who disappear from the weekly panel, which is not the same record as the experiment's quits. The Dataverse deposit needs a guestbook form; the ingest uses the authors' public copy of the same files, pinned by SHA-256. The frozen protocol is `docs/plans/ctrip_protocol.md`.
