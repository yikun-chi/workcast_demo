# Validation: AI adoption by sector (Census BTOS)

Numeric claims are generated by `scripts/build_adoption_validation.py` from `runs/btos_ai/evaluation.json`.

## Design

Rebuilds U.S. businesses as populations of firms, one population per sector. Each period a firm that does not use AI may start, and a firm that uses it may stop, with hazards that follow a diffusion curve: firms imitate adopters in their own sector and in the whole economy, and growth can slow as fewer non-users remain. From each cutoff the simulation carries every sector forward and is scored against the share of firms that actually reported AI use three and six months later. The alternative asks how much faster manufacturing and retail would adopt if an adoption programme doubled their firms' hazard of starting.

14 cutoffs from 2024-06-17 to 2025-06-16; the first 4 were used to make every design choice and the other 10 (2024-10-07 to 2025-06-16) were run once after the design was frozen in `docs/plans/btos_ai_protocol.md`. Targets run to 2025-09-08. Every prediction uses only periods before its cutoff (`tests/test_adoption.py` checks that deleting every later outcome leaves the prediction unchanged). The point prediction is the simulation's central path; ranges are the 5th-95th percentile of 20 runs per cutoff.

The outcome is the published survey estimate for each sector and period, with its own sampling error; sectors with mostly suppressed estimates are left out. Census changed the question's wording on 2025-11-17 and started a new series because of a level shift, so no period after that date is used.

## Alternative (simulated only)

A programme (training, subsidised tools) doubles the probability that a non-using manufacturing or retail firm starts using AI in each period. Every other sector, and the firms that already use AI, behave as in the simulation of what happened. The size of the effect is authored, not estimated.

At the end of the horizon, averaged over the last 2 cutoffs and 20 seeds in the affected groups (31, 44): 6.25 pp as it happened in the simulation, 7.53 pp with the alternative. This is a simulated alternative, not an observed change.

## Limitations

- The truth is a survey estimate: part of every error is sampling noise that no method can predict.
- Firms are simulated as populations of identical agents per sector; firm size and the survey weights are not modelled.
- The adoption programme alternative uses an authored hazard multiplier, not an estimated effect.
- Census data are public domain; the result can be shown to clients without licence restrictions.
- Retrospective in part: the world type and the comparators were written with these series visible. The held-out cutoffs were run once after the design was frozen on the development cutoffs; no design choice was made on them.
