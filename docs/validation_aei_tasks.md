# Validation: Which occupations' work goes to AI next (Anthropic Economic Index)

Numeric claims are generated by `scripts/build_adoption_validation.py` from `runs/aei_tasks/evaluation.json`.

## Design

Rebuilds Claude.ai use as 22 occupation groups, each a population of simulated workers whose use of the assistant follows a diffusion path; each group's share of conversations is its use relative to every other group's. From each release the simulation predicts every group's share in the next release, and is scored against what the next release reported. The alternative asks how the mix would shift if office and administrative workers took up the assistant twice as fast.

4 cutoffs from 2025-09-15 to 2026-06-26; the first 2 were used to make every design choice and the other 2 (2026-03-24 to 2026-06-26) were run once after the design was frozen in `docs/plans/aei_tasks_protocol.md`. Targets run to 2026-06-26. Every prediction uses only periods before its cutoff (`tests/test_adoption.py` checks that deleting every later outcome leaves the prediction unchanged). The point prediction is the simulation's central path; ranges are the 5th-95th percentile of 20 runs per cutoff.

Each release is reduced the same way: global Claude.ai shares of conversations by O*NET task, without none and not_classified, split equally across the occupations that list each task and summed by SOC major group. The classifier, sample windows and product scope change between releases (the June 2026 release adds Cowork conversations and reports calendar months), and every method has to predict across those changes. ## Alternative (simulated only)

An enterprise roll-out aimed at office and administrative support work doubles the probability that a non-using worker in that group starts using the assistant in each period. Every other group behaves as in the simulation of what happened; shares are rescaled to sum to 100%. The size of the change is authored, not estimated.

At the end of the horizon, averaged over the last 2 cutoffs and 20 seeds in the affected groups (43): 8.89 pp as it happened in the simulation, 8.89 pp with the alternative. This is a simulated alternative, not an observed change.

## Limitations

- Only two held-out releases; the result is indicative, not a stable estimate.
- Shares of conversations, not of workers or of work done: a group can gain share because other groups lose it.
- Releases are irregularly spaced and each is treated as one step.
- The office uptake alternative uses an authored hazard multiplier, not an estimated effect.
- Retrospective in part: the world type and the comparators were written with these series visible. The held-out cutoffs were run once after the design was frozen on the development cutoffs; no design choice was made on them.
